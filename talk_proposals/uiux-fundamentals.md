# Talk Proposal

## *Fundamentals of UI/UX: Lessons from Contemporary Good and Bad Design Examples*

### Abstract

Why does one app feel effortless while another leaves you frustrated? The answer lies in **User Interface (UI)** and **User Experience (UX)** design. UI shapes how a product looks and responds, while UX defines the overall journey of interaction. Together, they determine whether a product is intuitive, accessible, and engaging—or confusing and frustrating.  

This talk introduces the **fundamentals of UI/UX** through real-world examples of both good and bad design. Participants will explore key principles such as usability, accessibility, feedback, and consistency, while also touching on **emerging trends like AI-driven personalization, inclusive design, and sustainable practices**.  

By connecting design theory with practical case studies and interactive exercises, the session equips students to critique digital products, avoid common design pitfalls, and apply UI/UX principles to their own projects, research, or career paths.  

---

### Why This Talk Matters

Many students create websites, apps, or dashboards as part of their coursework. But **employers expect not just working apps, but apps people love to use**. In interviews, internships, and startup pitches, **UI/UX often becomes the difference between a selected and a rejected project**. 

Good design skills also extend far beyond computer science: management dashboards, data visualization, healthcare platforms, government portals all demand usable, and even public policy websites depend on user-centered design. By learning these fundamentals, students from diverse disciplines can communicate ideas more clearly, design more inclusive solutions, and build products that actually get adopted. 

This talk equips participants with both the mindset and the tools to recognize good design patterns, avoid common pitfalls, and apply design thinking across academic, professional, and entrepreneurial contexts.

---

### Talk Outline

1. **Introduction & Hook (10 min)**  
   * Provocative examples: Why some apps delight, others frustrate.  
   * Everyday design → digital design.

2. **Why UI/UX? (10 min)**  
   * Definitions of UI and UX, overlap and differences.  
   * Case study: a simple redesign showing user impact.  
   * Career relevance: why design skills matter.

3. **Core Principles (15 min)**  
   * Usability, feedback, consistency, aesthetics, visual hierarchy.  
   * **Accessibility:** WCAG basics, color contrast, designing for disabilities.  
   * Ethics: dark patterns, manipulative notifications, privacy concerns.

4. **Examples of Good Design (10 min)**  
   * *Figma* (collaborative design + simplicity).  
   * *Canva* (student-friendly).  
   * *Duolingo* (gamified onboarding).

5. **Examples of Bad Design (10 min)**  
   * Snapchat redesign backlash.  
   * Cluttered news portals overloaded with ads.  
   * Legacy IRCTC portal as a local context.

6. **Emerging Trends (10 min)**  
   * AI-driven personalization.  
   * Adaptive/chat-based UIs.  
   * Sustainability: low-bandwidth friendly, energy-efficient design.  
   * Cultural context in global design choices.

7. **Conclusion & Call to Action (5 min)**  
   * Start critiquing apps you use daily.
   * Join design communities, try redesign challenges.
   * Think of UI/UX as a career skill, not just an elective interest.

8. **Q&A + Reflection (15–20 min)**  
   * Students share their “bad design” finds.  
   * Open discussion on cross-disciplinary relevance.

---

### Audience Exercises

* **Find the Better Design (10 min):** Use live polls (Mentimeter/Slido) to vote on paired examples.  
* **Quick Redesign Challenge (10 min):** Groups sketch fixes for a flawed UI screenshot.  

(Allocate 15–20 minutes for exercises + discussion.)

---

### Duration

**Total Duration: 75–90 minutes**  
* 55–60 minutes: core content.  
* 15–20 minutes: exercises + Q&A.

---

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

* Distinguish UI vs UX and explain their role in product success.  
* Critique at least two digital products for usability and accessibility flaws.  
* Apply core design principles in projects or apps.  
* Recognize ethical and cultural issues in design.  
* Explore trends like AI-personalization and sustainability.  
* Connect UI/UX to career paths in tech, management, and research.

---

### Target Audience

* **Beginners:** Students curious about design.  
* **Intermediate learners:** Students experimenting with UI/UX.  
* **Tech enthusiasts:** Developers, product managers, or researchers who want to improve their design mindset.
