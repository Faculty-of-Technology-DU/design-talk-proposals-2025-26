# Talk Proposal

## *Fundamentals of UI/UX: Lessons from Contemporary Good and Bad Design Examples*


### Abstract

Design is not just decoration or aesthetics—it is how people experience and interact with the world; it is the language of connection. It shapes how we live, work, and engage with the spaces and tools around us: from the layout of a chair to the navigation of a mobile app. Every design decision carries intention, guiding choices and emotions, often without us realizing it. Good design enables us to achieve our goals effortlessly, while poor design creates frustration and confusion. A well-crafted experience—whether in technology, art, or daily life—harmonizes human psychology with the environment.

In today’s digital era, this principle comes alive through **User Interface (UI)** and **User Experience (UX)** design. UI defines how a product looks and responds, while UX shapes the entire journey of interaction. Together, they determine whether a product is intuitive, engaging, and accessible—or confusing and alienating.

This talk introduces the fundamentals of UI/UX in an approachable yet enriching way. Through contemporary examples of both **good and bad design**, participants will explore core principles such as usability, accessibility, feedback, consistency, and aesthetics. The session also highlights emerging trends and demonstrates how UI/UX bridges creative and technical worlds, providing a foundation and inspiration for students to engage with design as a critical part of technology, research, and innovation.


### Why This Talk Matters

Many students build websites, apps, or dashboards as part of academic projects. Yet, functionality alone does not guarantee success—a technically working system can still frustrate and overwhelm users if it ignores good design principles. With industries increasingly prioritizing **user-centered design**, this knowledge is vital for students preparing for internships, startups, or professional roles.

This session will help participants recognize good design patterns, avoid common pitfalls, and appreciate UI/UX as a critical factor in product success. Beyond best practices, it emphasizes that great designers plan not only for smooth interactions but also for **worst-case scenarios**—anticipating confusion, errors, and frustrations, and designing ways to guide users through them.


### Talk Outline

1. **Introduction to Design (10 min)**  
   * Everyday good/bad design examples (physical + digital).  
   * Why design is not decoration, but problem-solving.

2. **Why UI/UX? (10 min)**  
   * Transition from general design to digital design.  
   * What is UI? What is UX? How they differ + overlap.  
   * Beginner-friendly case study: a simple app redesign showing impact.

3. **Core Principles of UI/UX (15 min)**  
   * Usability, accessibility, consistency, feedback, visual hierarchy, aesthetics.  
   * Clear explanations with relatable examples.

4. **Contemporary Good Examples (10 min)**  
   * *Duolingo* (gamified onboarding).  
   * *Notion* (clean, flexible, intuitive workspace).  
   * *Apple.com* (clear hierarchy, strong visuals).

5. **Contemporary Bad Examples (10 min)**  
   * *IRCTC’s older website* (cluttered, poor navigation).  
   * Legacy government portals (slow, unresponsive).  
   * Apps overloaded with ads/pop-ups.

6. **Emerging Trends & Tech-Design Intersection (10 min)**  
   * Minimalism, personalization, dark mode, inclusive/ethical design.  
   * How design connects with coding, research, and innovation.

7. **Conclusion & Path Forward (5 min)**  
   * How beginners can start learning UI/UX.  
   * How experienced students can refine their path.

8. **Q\&A and Reflection (15 min)**  
   * Open discussion where students share their own “bad design” finds.


### Audience Exercises

* **Find the Better Design (10 min):** Students compare paired interfaces, critique strengths/weaknesses, and debate why one works better.  
* **Match the Elements (10 min):** A blank webpage layout with numbered placeholders; students must correctly place UI elements, reinforcing layout and hierarchy.


### Duration

**Total Duration: 75 minutes**  

* 55–60 minutes: Interactive presentation with visuals and discussions  
* 15–20 minutes: Audience exercises and open Q\&A


### Learning Objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

* Appreciate the role of design in everyday life and digital interactions.  
* Distinguish between UI and UX and see their practical impact.  
* Identify and apply the **core principles** of effective design.  
* Critically analyze real-world examples of good and bad UI/UX.  
* Explore emerging trends and ethical considerations.  
* Recognize how design and technology intersect, opening pathways for future learning or careers.


### Target Audience

* **Beginners/newcomers:** Students curious about design but with no prior exposure.  
* **Design learners:** Students already experimenting with design who want more direction.  
* **Tech enthusiasts:** Students from technical fields interested in understanding the design side of technology.

This ensures participants at all levels will gain both **fundamental knowledge** and **new exposure** to how design and technology intersect.
